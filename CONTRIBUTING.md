# Contributing to the Caring Sensor Community

Welcome. We're glad you're here.

This document explains how to contribute to the CSC foundation repository — our shared record of thinking, decisions, and direction. Product code repositories have their own contributing guides; links will be added as those repositories are created.

---

## Who Should Contribute Here

This repository is for people who want to:
- Help shape how we think about the problem
- Improve our documentation and community resources
- Propose changes to our governance or mission documents
- Share knowledge from caregiving, design, development, or research experience

You do not need to be a developer to contribute. Most of what lives here is plain text — markdown documents that anyone can read and edit through GitHub's web interface.

---

## The Basics

### What is a Pull Request?

A pull request (PR) is how you propose a change. Think of it as saying: "Here is what I think should change, and here is why." The maintainers review it, discuss it if needed, and either merge it or explain why not.

Every contribution — from fixing a typo to proposing a governance change — follows the same path.

### How to Make Your First Contribution

1. **Find something to improve.** Browse the documents. If something is unclear, missing, or wrong — that's a contribution.

2. **Edit on GitHub.** Open the file you want to change. Click the pencil icon (Edit this file). Make your change in the browser. No software to install.

3. **Describe your change.** At the bottom of the edit page, write a short description of what you changed and why. One or two sentences is enough.

4. **Submit a pull request.** GitHub will walk you through it. You'll see a button that says "Propose changes" — click it.

5. **Wait for review.** Someone from the team will look at your PR, usually within a week. We may ask questions or suggest adjustments. That's normal — it's how the work gets better.

## What We're Looking For

**Improvements to existing documents** — clarity, accuracy, completeness.

**New resources for the community** — templates, guides, research summaries, case studies from your own caregiving or care technology experience.

**Questions that should be in a FAQ** — if you had a question we didn't answer, someone else will too. Open an issue and we'll work on it together.

**Corrections** — if something we've written is wrong, please tell us. We'd rather be corrected than confidently incorrect.

---

## What We're Not Looking For (Right Now)

- Product code (that lives in separate repositories when they're ready)
- Unsolicited redesigns of our visual direction
- Marketing copy or promotional content
- Contributions that require us to take a position on contested political or policy questions

---

## Community Norms

**Be specific.** Vague feedback is hard to act on. "This section is confusing" is less useful than "This section assumes the reader already knows what a plugin is."

**Disagree by proposing an alternative.** Don't just say something is wrong — show what right looks like.

**Assume good faith.** Everyone contributing here cares about the same problem. Disagreements about approach are welcome. Disrespect is not.

**Ask before investing.** If you're planning a large contribution — a new document, a significant structural change — open an issue first and check that it's wanted before doing the work.

---

## A Note on AI-Assisted Contributions

We use Claude and other AI tools as part of our workflow. Contributions that were developed with AI assistance are welcome, with one expectation: you have read it, understood it, and stand behind it. Your name on a contribution means you take responsibility for it, regardless of how it was drafted.
