# CSC Fictional Names Policy

*Part of the CSC Style Notes family · Last updated: May 2026*

---

## Purpose

When CSC creates documentation, scenarios, website stories, or educational materials, we use fictional people to illustrate real situations. A grandmother who needs overnight monitoring. A family member managing care from another city. A community member setting up a sensor network for the first time.

These fictional people deserve real names — names that reflect the actual diversity of people who age at home, receive care, and give it.

This policy establishes how we build and use our fictional names list, inspired by a practice at Microsoft where employees consent to have their names used in documentation. We've adapted it to fit a community organization where the people whose names appear may one day read the stories they're in.

---

## The list

The CSC fictional names list is a standing roster of first names (and optionally last names) contributed by community members who have consented to their use. Names on the list:

- May appear in any CSC documentation, website content, scenario examples, or educational materials
- Are used to represent fictional characters only — never to attribute real statements, opinions, or actions to real people
- Carry no other meaning about the contributor — a name may be assigned to any role, age, or situation the story requires

The list is maintained in this repository at `community/fictional-names.md`.

---

## Consent

Consent is simple and covers all fictional uses. There are no restrictions on role, scenario, or character type.

**Current process (early stage):** The founder asks community members personally. Anyone who wants to participate can also email [caringsensorcommunity@gmail.com](mailto:caringsensorcommunity@gmail.com) with "Names List" in the subject line — Mark will follow up. A note is kept in `community/fictional-names.md` recording who consented and when.

**Future process (as community grows):** A checkbox at community signup: *"I'd like my name on the CSC fictional names list — it may appear in documentation and stories as a fictional character."*

Consent may be withdrawn at any time by contacting the community steward. Withdrawn names will be retired from future use; existing published documents are updated on a best-effort basis.

---

## Using the list

When writing documentation or stories that need a person's name:

1. **Choose from the list** rather than inventing a name ad hoc.
2. **Favor demographic variety.** The list should reflect the real population of people who give and receive home care — which is older, majority female, and ethnically diverse. Draw from across the list, not just the familiar names at the top.
3. **Avoid patterns.** Don't consistently assign certain names to certain roles (e.g., always a female care recipient, always a male technician). Vary freely.
4. **"Maria" is grandfathered.** The name Maria, used in CSC's founding website story, is an existing fictional name. It is considered on the list by virtue of its established use.

---

## When a real name is used

Mark Shea is named as CSC's founder in official documents and the public record. His name is used as himself, not as a fictional character, and this policy does not apply to that use.

For all other real, named individuals — board members, collaborators, partners — names appear only with their knowledge and in accurate context.

---

## If someone objects

If a person ever contacts CSC to say they are uncomfortable with their name appearing in our materials — whether or not they consented — we will:

1. Thank them for reaching out.
2. Explain the consent process and its origins.
3. Retire their name from the list immediately.
4. Update published materials that use their name on a best-effort basis.

No questions asked. The relationship matters more than the name.

---

## A note on why this matters

Names are not neutral. A documentation ecosystem where every example person is named "John" or "Mary" quietly signals who the community imagines itself serving. Our names list is a small, concrete way to make the invitation legible: this community is for everyone who cares for someone, and everyone who needs care.

We'd love for community members to watch for their name to appear in a story someday. That's the point.
