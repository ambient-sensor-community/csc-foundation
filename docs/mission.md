# Mission & Philosophy

**Last updated:** May 2026  
**Status:** Working draft — open for community input

---

## Mission

To develop, evaluate, and share practical approaches to homecare sensor networks that help people remain safely at home while strengthening trust, consent, accessibility, and human connection.

---

## The Problem We're Solving

Millions of families face a version of the same tension: wanting to know that someone they love is okay at home, without surveilling them, alarming them, or reducing them to a data point.

The tools available today are either clinical (medical alert systems designed for crisis, not daily life), creepy (broad-spectrum surveillance dressed up as care), or both. None of them were built by people who have actually sat with a family trying to navigate this.

We are building something different.

---

## What We Believe

**Care is a pattern recognition problem, not a monitoring problem.**

The goal is not to watch. The goal is to notice — quietly, over time — when something shifts. A morning routine that changed. A meal that was skipped. A neighbor who stopped dropping by. Small things that, seen early and understood calmly, allow the people who matter to respond thoughtfully.

**The people closest to the problem build the best tools.**

Caregivers know things that no product team has ever discovered in a focus group. Families who have lived through a difficult stretch at home understand what would have helped in ways that no technologist has imagined. Our model is built on getting those people into the room — or the repository — and taking what they know seriously.

**Privacy is architecture, not a policy.**

Local-first data is not a feature. It is a structural commitment. Care data stays in the home. It is never sold, never aggregated for advertising, never used to train commercial models without explicit consent. This is not negotiable and it is not a marketing position. It is how the system is built.

**Sensors advise. People decide.**

Technology in this space has a tendency to become authoritative — to issue alerts, assign risk scores, recommend interventions. We reject that model. The sensor notices. The family interprets. The person at home remains the subject of care, not the object of monitoring.

## What We Are Building

### The Family Feed

A vertically stacked timeline of subtle daily signals — kitchen activity, walks completed, check-ins from family members and neighbors — interpreted by a quiet AI voice that reads patterns over time rather than issuing alerts in the moment.

The AI Pattern Summary speaks the way a thoughtful person would: observational, calm, temporal. "Morning looked familiar. There was a quieter stretch around midday — nothing concerning, but worth a gentle check-in later this week."

Never an alarm. Never a score. Never a percentage.

### The Plugin Ecosystem (V2)

The core platform is the cathedral — stable, trusted, maintained. The community builds the bazaar: plugins that contribute signals to the feed, care templates that codify what experienced caregivers know, workflows that reflect the real texture of how families actually operate.

This is the long-term structural advantage. No single company can build everything. A community of people who care can.

### The Certification Program

Local experts — technologists, caregivers, community members — who can walk into someone's home, configure the system respectfully, explain the privacy model clearly, and be trusted in that space. Certification is both a revenue model and a distribution network. Twenty certified experts in twenty communities, each serving twenty-five families, is a real and human-scale beginning.
