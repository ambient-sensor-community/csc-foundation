# CSC Style Notes

**Primary reference:** Chicago Manual of Style (CMOS, 18th ed.)  
**Technical reference:** Google Developer Documentation Style Guide  
**When they conflict:** CMOS prevails.

---

## Punctuation

- **Oxford comma.** Always use the serial comma: "trust, consent, and connection."
- **Em dashes.** No spaces on either side—like this.
- **Quotation marks.** Double marks for quotations; single marks for quotations within quotations.
- **Ellipses.** Three spaced periods . . . with a space before and after.

---

## Capitalization

- **Titles.** Headline-style for titles; sentence-style for captions and subtitles.
- **internet, web, email.** Lowercase always. Not "the Internet" or "the Web."
- **Organization name.** Caring Sensor Community (full); CSC (abbreviated after first use).
- **Job titles.** Lowercase when used descriptively: "the founder, Mark Shea." Capitalize before a name: "Founder Mark Shea."

---

## Numbers

- **Spell out one through one hundred** in running text; use numerals for 101 and above.
- **Percentages.** Use the % symbol in running text: "35% of households."
- **Dates.** Month Day, Year format: "May 26, 2026." No ordinals (not "May 26th").
- **Never start a sentence with a numeral.** Rewrite or spell out.

---

## Technology terms

- **Sensor network** (two words, no hyphen).
- **Open source** (adjective, no hyphen): "open source software." *(Google Dev Style)*
- **GitHub, Home Assistant.** Use exact product capitalization always.
- **Code and commands** in monospace in all technical documents. Follow Google Developer Style Guide for CLI formatting.

---

## Voice and tone

- **Plain language first.** If a shorter word works, use it.
- **Active voice preferred.** "The sensor detects motion" not "Motion is detected by the sensor."
- **Avoid jargon** in community-facing writing. Define technical terms on first use.
- **Person-first language** always: "a person who uses a wheelchair," not "a wheelchair user."

---

## Notes on key decisions

**"data is" (singular):** CMOS treats *data* as a mass noun in general writing. This will feel natural in community-facing content, though researchers may prefer the plural. The singular form is a deliberate house choice.

**"homecare" (one word):** Treated as a settled domain term rather than a compound modifier.

**Person-first language:** The caregiving and aging-in-place context favors person-first. Be aware that some disability advocates prefer identity-first language ("disabled person"); this is a living conversation worth monitoring.
